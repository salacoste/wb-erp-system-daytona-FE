## What Went Well

### 1. The rule recursion-termination mechanism was empirically discovered

Story 113.2 codified the **block-level blanket qualifier** as the rule's natural termination mechanism for Trigger 4 recursion. The pattern emerged organically:
- Pass 1: Story 113.2 ships Trigger 4; reviewer hunts for meta-claims; finds 4 unqualified ones (Trigger 4 fires).
- Pass 2: 1st-pass fixes add "unaudited meta-claim" qualifiers per-claim, but introduce NEW unqualified meta-claims (Trigger 4 fires again).
- Pass 3 (proposed): block-level blanket qualifier — one declaration at the top of the Post-Nth-pass block covers all meta-claims in that block, eliminating in-line repetition. **Trigger 4 stops firing.**
- Pass 4 confirmed: the Post-3rd-pass block lacked its own qualifier; Trigger 4 fired one more time before terminating. The fix: extend blanket qualifier convention to all future Post-Nth-pass blocks.

The block-level blanket qualifier is now a documented convention (Post-1st-pass + Post-2nd-pass + Post-3rd-pass + Post-4th-pass blocks all carry their own). Future stories making heavy use of meta-claim language can adopt this convention up-front to avoid Trigger 4 escalation.

### 2. 4-pass empirical bound reproduced (3rd independent observation)

Stories 112.3 (26 findings), 112.4 (25), 113.2 (18) all converged on **4-pass chain length** despite a 1.4× difference in finding density. Story 113.1 also ran 4-pass. **4 out of 4 most recent novel-pattern / discipline-codification stories converged on 4 passes.** This suggests the multi-pass discipline has a natural fixed point around 4 passes for stories whose narratives contain recursive self-referential meta-claims.

Trigger 3's `>5 findings → (N+1)th-pass MANDATORY` rule appears well-calibrated: every pass past the 4th has surfaced ≤5 findings in the observed corpus, terminating the chain. Recommend keeping the >5 threshold for at least 2 more discipline-codification stories before considering tuning.

### 3. Recursion-termination claim itself audited

The most striking empirical outcome: 3rd-pass narratives that claimed "X terminates" or "X does not fire" were themselves unaudited until a fresh-context 4th-pass scrutinized them. This is Story 97.4-FE structural permanence at the deepest meta-level yet observed — author confidence about their own meta-observation IS the unaudited claim. The user-invoked 4th-passes (both `/code-review 113.1` AND `/code-review 113.2`) caught substantive findings the 3rd-pass narrative confidently declared resolved.

**Implication**: even when Trigger 3 numerically terminates escalation (≤5), one additional fresh-context pass is warranted IF the 3rd-pass narrative made meta-claims about termination/escalation status. This is the empirical justification for Trigger 4's RECOMMENDED disposition: it's the rule's self-application to its own recursion.

### 4. Documentation-only epic preserved all source code invariants

Both stories were docs-only (CLAUDE.md + CLAUDE-PATTERNS.md edits). Zero source code changes. Vitest passing count unchanged (7994). Self-test unchanged (23/23). ESLint baseline unchanged (0E/112w). Type-check unchanged (0). The discipline can be codified without touching shipped code — and this epic proves the codification step is itself bounded (2 stories, 8 passes, 37 findings, 0 production impact).

### 5. APPEND-ONLY convention scaled to 4-pass narratives

Both stories accumulated 4 Post-Nth-pass-review blocks each. The APPEND-ONLY rule (Story 111.1-FE F-2) held throughout: prior Post-pass blocks + close-row Lessons were NEVER edited in-place; corrections happened via new Post-Nth-pass blocks + new dated disclosure rows. Story 113.2's 4th-pass specifically tested the convention by NOT editing the "47 lines" attestation in the close-row (now actually 48); the disclosure was added separately.

### 6. Baseline integrity preserved through 8 review passes

`scripts/.check-docs-baseline.txt` never modified across 2 stories × 4 passes × multi-fix-cycles = ~30+ edit opportunities. The reflex "don't ratchet baseline" trained in Epic 112 held throughout Epic 113.

## What Was Challenging

### 1. Stories codifying rules systematically violate the rules they codify (3rd structural reconfirmation)

This is Story 97.4-FE codification's predicted outcome reconfirmed at scale. Both Story 113.1 AND Story 113.2 manifested the very defect classes their own Lessons warned about, within the same review chain:
- Story 113.1 codified fix-block propagation discipline → had 5 fix-block propagation failures across its 4 passes
- Story 113.2 codified Trigger 4 (meta-claim escalation) → had Trigger 4 fire 3 times in passes 1+2+3

The structural reason: meta-claims and propagation are exactly the kinds of attestation work that cannot be audited within the pass that generated them. Author memory is systematically inadequate; the only countermeasure is multi-pass adversarial review with fresh context. Epic 113-FE reproduces this finding across 8 review passes — strengthening the empirical case for Story 97.4-FE's structural-permanence claim.

### 2. Recursion-termination requires explicit user/coordinator intervention

Trigger 3 numerically terminates escalation at ≤5 findings. But Trigger 4's RECOMMENDED disposition leaves termination to coordinator judgment. In practice, BOTH stories' 4th-passes were user-invoked (`/code-review 113.1` + `/code-review 113.2`) — neither was triggered by the coordinator's interpretation of Trigger 4. Without user intervention, both stories would have closed at 3-pass with unaudited recursion-termination claims.

Implication: Trigger 4 may need promotion to MANDATORY for novel-pattern stories whose 3rd-pass narrative makes meta-claims about Trigger 4 itself (recursive escalation of the recursive escalation). Defer to Epic 114-FE judgment.

### 3. Block-level blanket qualifier coverage scope ambiguity

The first blanket qualifier (Post-1st-pass block) said "this block uses..." — ambiguous whether "this block" meant just the Post-1st-pass block or all subsequent blocks. The 2nd-pass added a separate qualifier to the Post-2nd-pass block; 3rd-pass added one to Post-3rd-pass; 4th-pass added one to Post-4th-pass. Each block needed its OWN qualifier — the convention doesn't propagate forward.

This is verbose but defensible: each block is independent narrative; each pass adds new meta-claims; each pass must explicitly qualify. Future stories adopting the pattern should pre-write a qualifier template for each anticipated Post-pass block.

### 4. Cumulative finding count attestation is itself a propagation defect

Story 113.2 sprint-status comment claimed "15 cumulative findings — 6+5+4" until the 4th-pass surfaced 3 more, requiring update to "18 — 6+5+4+3". This is a chronological propagation issue: any cumulative-count attestation in narrative is stale the moment the next pass runs. Future stories should either (a) omit cumulative counts from narrative until close, or (b) explicit "(post-Nth-pass)" qualifier on every cumulative figure.

## Key Insights

### Insight 1: Multi-pass discipline has a natural 4-pass fixed point

4 of 4 most recent novel-pattern / discipline-codification stories (Stories 112.3, 112.4, 113.1, 113.2) converged on 4-pass chain length regardless of finding density. Trigger 3's `>5 findings` threshold appears well-calibrated for the current codebase's complexity. The 4-pass empirical bound may not generalize to fundamentally different work (e.g., large refactors); revisit when those occur.

### Insight 2: The rule catches itself recursively at every iteration

Story 113.2's narrative recursive structure ("Trigger 4 fires on the story that codified Trigger 4") generalizes: every rule shipped in CLAUDE.md catches its own violations on first attempt — by design, not failure. The rule operates correctly at the meta-level by accident of the codification's structure. This is intellectually satisfying but practically demanding: every discipline-codification story should expect to fully self-demonstrate the rule it ships before close.

### Insight 3: User-invoked 4th-passes are empirically valuable

Both `/code-review 113.1` AND `/code-review 113.2` (user-invoked post-close) surfaced substantive findings the 3rd-pass narrative confidently declared resolved. Story 112.4's user-invoked 4th-pass had the same outcome (caught 2 recursive self-violations from 3rd-pass fixes). **3 of 3 user-invoked post-close 4th-passes have surfaced HIGH or MEDIUM severity findings.** This is strong evidence that the "supernumerary" 4th-pass is actually valuable — recommend keeping `/code-review <story>` as a default post-close action for discipline-codification stories.

### Insight 4: Block-level blanket qualifiers preserve narrative readability

Without the blanket-qualifier convention, every "self-violation" / "self-codified" / "MANDATORY" phrase would need its own in-line `(unaudited meta-claim)` qualifier. The Post-1st-pass block alone would balloon by 30-50% in length. The block-level convention preserves readability while satisfying Trigger 4.

### Insight 5: Documentation-only epics can preserve all invariants

Epic 113-FE is the first epic in the codebase (within the 60+ streak) to ship ZERO source code changes. All gates held at baseline. This proves the multi-pass discipline can be applied to pure-documentation work without touching the production codebase. Future epics that need only doc updates can confidently use this pattern.
