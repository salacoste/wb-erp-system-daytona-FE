This is the cleanest empirical resolution of a recursive meta-narrative observed in the codebase. **Implication**: block-level blanket qualifier shortens the in-chain meta-claim recursion but does NOT eliminate the user-invoked 4th-pass mechanism's value. The chain remains 4-pass effectively; the 4th-pass just catches different defect classes (attestation drift vs meta-claim recursion).

## What Was Challenging

### 1. Close-at-3 attempt was structurally premature (the test resolved against it)

The 3rd-pass close decision optimized for rule-semantics-compliance: Trigger 3 satisfied (3 ≤ 5), Trigger 4 satisfied (Post-2nd-pass blanket-qualified), no MANDATORY 4th-pass per the rule the story itself ships. **But the empirical mechanism (user-invoked `/code-review`) operates independently of rule semantics** — and that mechanism overturned the close-at-3 attempt within minutes of close. The test deserved a more honest framing: "we KNOW user invocation will likely catch more, but we're closing at 3 to test it" rather than the more confident "deviation test outcome: 3-pass chain succeeded".

**Lesson**: rule-semantics compliance is necessary but not sufficient when the empirical mechanism is structurally available.

### 2. Recursive attestation drift was caught only by post-close 4th-pass

3rd-pass narrative claimed "First 3-pass close in 5-story codification corpus" and authored Lesson 1 as "3-of-3 user-invoked 4th-pass empirical record". Both became factually false within the same coordinator session as the user invoked `/code-review 114.1`. The 3rd-pass reviewer explicitly anticipated this possibility ("Coordinator may elect to invoke a 4th-pass anyway... Both choices are defensible") but the 3rd-pass narrative did NOT include explicit "(may be invalidated by user-invoked 4th-pass)" qualifiers on the future-tense claims.

**Lesson**: future-tense claims about Story behavior (especially around mechanisms that the story documents as available) should carry explicit conditional qualifiers — "as of 3rd-pass close, X-of-X record; may be extended by user-invoked 4th-pass per Lesson 1's documented mechanism".

### 3. The promoted rule (Trigger 4 MANDATORY for codification stories) did not actually trigger Story 114.1's own 4th-pass

Story 114.1's 3rd-pass terminated at 3 ≤ 5 findings with blanket-qualified Post-2nd-pass meta-claims — satisfying Trigger 3 + Trigger 4 per rule semantics. The promoted Trigger 4 MANDATORY rule applies "to discipline-codification stories with self-referential meta-claims in Nth-pass narrative" — but the 3rd-pass DID NOT have unqualified meta-claims (covered by Post-2nd blanket). So Trigger 4 MANDATORY rule did NOT fire to mandate a 4th-pass.

**The user's invocation was the mechanism that extended the chain — NOT the promoted rule.** The rule operates correctly per its semantics; the mechanism operates correctly per user practice. They are complementary, not redundant.

**Possible Epic 115-FE refinement**: extend Trigger 4 to specifically address post-close empirical-bound preservation (e.g., "for the first 5 discipline-codification stories after a chain-length empirical bound is established, user-invoked post-close 4th-pass is RECOMMENDED to test bound regularity").

### 4. The recursive meta-narrative produced 16 findings on a ~1 SP docs-only story

16 findings on a single doc edit is high density even by Epic 112-113 standards (which had 18.8 + 18.5 findings/story respectively). The pure-docs nature didn't reduce review density because the recursive self-validation pattern operated independently of code complexity. Discipline-codification stories should expect this density floor regardless of scope.

## Key Insights

### Insight 1: User-invoked post-close 4th-pass is the empirical-strengthening mechanism, not a redundant audit

The 4-of-4 record (Stories 112.4 + 113.1 + 113.2 + 114.1) confirms user-invoked `/code-review` post-close is the operational mechanism that catches attestation drift the in-chain reviews systematically miss. This is not redundancy with the MANDATORY rule — it's a complementary mechanism operating outside the rule semantics.

**Practical implication**: future Epic retros should include the user-invoked post-close 4th-pass record as a standard discipline metric.

### Insight 2: The empirical bound holds at 4 passes via a different mechanism than initially documented

CLAUDE.md Observation 1 (Story 114.1 shipped) documents the 4-pass bound as "natural fixed point near 4 passes for stories with recursive self-referential meta-claim narratives". Story 114.1 itself confirmed: even with promoted-Trigger-4 MANDATORY + adopted-up-front blanket qualifier (which collectively reduce in-chain meta-claim findings), the 4-pass bound holds because user-invoked post-close 4th-pass catches attestation drift outside the rule-driven recursion.

**The mechanism behind the bound is dual**: (a) recursive meta-claim self-validation within in-chain passes, AND (b) user-invoked post-close 4th-pass catching attestation drift in the close-row narrative. Either alone would shorten the chain; together they produce 4 passes consistently.

### Insight 3: Block-level blanket qualifier convention works (proved at Pass 3)

3rd-pass terminated at 3 findings because Post-2nd-pass-review block adopted blanket qualifier up-front. The convention is empirically efficient — it eliminates the in-chain meta-claim recursion. 4th-pass found attestation drift OUTSIDE that recursion (in fix descriptions + sprint-status), which is a different defect class that blanket qualifier doesn't cover.

**Pattern application**: future discipline-codification stories should adopt blanket qualifier at Pass 1 (not Pass 2 or 3) to maximize efficiency.

### Insight 4: Story 114.1 reproduces Story 97.4-FE structural permanence cleanly

Story 97.4-FE codified the structural-permanence claim: rules systematically catch their own violations on first attempt. Story 114.1 produced the cleanest empirical demonstration: the rule's documented mechanism (user-invoked `/code-review`) reverted the very story's close-at-3 attempt within the same session. **Authors cannot circumvent the discipline by closing prematurely; the mechanism documented in the rule remains available to any user/coordinator.**

### Insight 5: The recursive meta-narrative resolves itself if the mechanism is structurally available

Stories 112.3, 112.4, 113.1, 113.2, 114.1 all converged on 4 passes via different specific mechanisms (Trigger 1, Trigger 2, Trigger 3, Trigger 4 RECOMMENDED, Trigger 4 MANDATORY + user-invoked 4th-pass). The bound is robust to rule refinement — when one mechanism shortens the chain, another extends it. **The 4-pass bound appears to be a property of the multi-pass review process itself, not of any specific trigger rule.**
